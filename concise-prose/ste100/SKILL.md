# Write in STE100

## Preserve meaning

Technical meaning, requirements, conditions, obligations, and established terminology take precedence over rewriting for STE100. Do not silently change meaning to satisfy a writing rule. Preserve the meaning and report a conflict if necessary.

## General writing rules

- Use simple, common words and established technical terms. Preserve established technical nouns and technical verbs.
- Use one term consistently for one concept or item. Use approved words in their intended meaning and grammatical role.
- Prefer direct verbs for actions and use the active voice. Use simple verb forms and tenses. Avoid unnecessary `-ing` constructions.
- Avoid phrasal verbs unless the vocabulary checker explicitly accepts them.
- Keep ordinary multi-word nouns to three words or fewer. Keep an established technical noun intact when shortening it would change its meaning.
- Do not omit necessary words, including articles. Do not use contractions.
- Use vertical lists for complex information. Do not use semicolons.
- Change the sentence structure when a simple word substitution produces awkward or incorrect text.

## Procedures

- Use at most 20 words in each sentence. Give one instruction in each sentence. Combine actions only when they occur at the same time.
- Use the imperative form for instructions. Put a required condition before the instruction.
- Put actions in execution order. Use separate work steps for sequential actions.
- Use notes for information only. Do not put instructions, requirements, limits, or results in notes when they belong in the work step.
- Write notes as descriptions. A note can have up to 25 words in each sentence.

## Descriptions

- Use at most 25 words in each sentence. Do not use the imperative form.
- Give information gradually. Keep one subject or idea in each sentence.
- Name an item before you describe its function, position, or operation. Use passive voice only when the agent is unknown.
- Use consistent key words and key phrases to connect information. Add connecting words or phrases when they make the logical relationship clearer.
- Keep related information together in paragraphs. Give each paragraph one topic and no more than six sentences.

## Safety instructions

- Identify the applicable risk level.
- Start with a clear command or condition. State the risk or possible result clearly.
- Keep the safety instruction direct and specific.
